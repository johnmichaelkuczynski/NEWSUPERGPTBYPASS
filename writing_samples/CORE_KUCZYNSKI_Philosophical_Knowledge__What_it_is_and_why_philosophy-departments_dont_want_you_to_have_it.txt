=== QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS ===

Q1: What is the main role of philosophy according to Kuczynski?
A1: Philosophy provides justification for the empirically unjustifiable assumptions on which sciences depend.

Q2: How does Kuczynski describe an empirical discipline?
A2: An empirical discipline's findings are justified by their consistency with sensory observation.

Q3: Why can't empirical disciplines justify all their assumptions?
A3: Because they rely on sensory information, which cannot be self-corroborated.

Q4: What role does a philosopher play in relation to scientific assumptions?
A4: Philosophers seek to provide the necessary philosophical justification for these assumptions.

Q5: Why does philosophy face more suspicion than mathematics?
A5: Because philosophy is unconventional in its methods and not directly connected to empirical results, unlike mathematics.

Q6: How do sense perceptions contribute to knowledge beyond their content?
A6: They can trigger ratiocinative processes that yield additional, non-perceptual knowledge.

Q7: What misconception about knowledge does Kuczynski aim to clarify?
A7: That all legitimate knowledge is empirical, dismissing the non-empirical sources like philosophy.

Q8: How does Kuczynski argue against the belief that there's no philosophical truth?
A8: By explaining that the statement is self-defeating; if true, it's also false.

Q9: What does Kuczynski say about agreement among philosophers?
A9: There is significant agreement on foundational issues despite surface-level disputes.

Q10: What are the key reasons for disagreements among philosophers according to the text?
A10: They often arise from personal egos and social dynamics rather than the discipline's logic.

Q11: Why might psychological factors affect philosophical progress?
A11: Due to the difficulty in distinguishing genuine philosophical talent and adapting to peer pressure.

Q12: What example does Kuczynski give of institutional conformity in philosophy?
A12: The case of Boris Kment adopting his mentor David Lewis’s modal realism uncritically.

Q13: What critique does Kuczynski levitate against Wittgenstein's philosophy?
A13: That it wrongly denies the independent existence of thoughts and meanings.

Q14: How does Kuczynski refute the view that thinking is not a mental process?
A14: By providing logical explanations and rejecting Wittgenstein's behaviorist implications.

Q15: What stance does Kuczynski take on the meaning of statements?
A15: Statements do have meanings, and understanding this is crucial for affirming propositions.

Q16: What fundamental assumption does Kuczynski challenge with his work?
A16: The assumption that empirical sciences can operate independently of philosophy.

Q17: What does Kuczynski suggest about the future of philosophy departments?
A17: They need reform to encourage genuine inquiry and reduce the influence of orthodoxies.

Q18: Why does Kuczynski mention 'method actors' versus British actors?
A18: To contrast different philosophical approaches to understanding roles, akin to philosophical inquiry.

Q19: What role does Kuczynski assign to empirical disciplines in knowledge acquisition?
A19: They require a philosophical framework to justify the non-empirical assumptions they rely on.

Q20: What solution does Kuczynski propose for philosophical education challenges?
A20: Encouraging independent thought and critical examination of traditional doctrines.

=== QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS ===
Q1: Does referring to Socrates require specific knowledge about him?
A1: Yes, it requires knowledge of a uniquely individuating description that applies solely to Socrates, even if the person referring isn't aware of all propositions true of Socrates.

Q2: Can one think about an object without a unique description applicable to it?
A2: No, the essay argues that both reference and thought require knowledge of a uniquely individuating description.

Q3: What is Kripke's main proposition regarding proper names?
A3: Kripke argues that proper names are not synonymous with definite descriptions but that names still rely on a causal chain connected to the referent.

Q4: What role do causal relations play in naming?
A4: Causal relations facilitate the transmission and application of names but are embedded within knowledge of a uniquely individuating description.

Q5: How does this text critique direct reference theory?
A5: While acknowledging the correctness of direct reference, the text asserts that epistemologically, knowledge of a uniquely identifying description is still necessary.

Q6: What is exemplified by "picking up" a name like "Argo"?
A6: It shows that even without direct or descriptive definition, context and conversational cues allow acquisition of uniquely individuating descriptions indirectly.

Q7: Can ostensive definitions stand alone without descriptions?
A7: No, ostensive definitions collapse into descriptive definitions because identifying an object requires describing it particularly from the context.

Q8: How do names like "Socrates" maintain their referential integrity over time?
A8: Through transmission that integrates uniquely individuating descriptions albeit with phonetic or contextual mutation.

Q9: Why might one forget the initial context of learning a name?
A9: Over time, descriptions that interlock, linking past to present contexts, replace initial knowledge, maintaining referential ability without specific memory of the origin.

Q10: What critical semantic-semantics distinction does the author make?
A10: The semantic rule of a name differs from the way it is learned; names label, but learning involves existential descriptions.
